Mail merge for Gmail, run entirely in your browser.

Upload a CSV, write one message with fields like {{first_name}}, check each person’s copy, then send from your own Gmail account. Free and open source.

Start sending Read the source on GitHub

You’ll sign in with Google. No account to create, nothing to install.

Three steps

Recipients, message, review. You can go back to any step before anything is sent.

  1. 1

    Recipients

    Upload a CSV with an email column, plus any columns you want in the message, like first_name or company. Up to 5,000 rows. Invalid addresses are flagged and won’t be sent.

    contacts.csv42 rows
    email column found
  2. 2

    Message

    Write once, and click a column name to drop in a field like {{company}}. Attach files if you need to. The preview beside the editor shows each person’s copy as you type.

    Hi {{first_name}}, Fields: email, first_name, last_name, company
  3. 3

    Review & send

    Step through every recipient’s email, then send from your Gmail. Messages go out one at a time, 250 ms apart, and any that fail can be retried.

    Recipient 1 of 42
    250 ms between sends

Your list stays between your browser and Google.

Automailr is a set of static files. There’s no Automailr server or database, so there’s nowhere for a copy of your data to end up.

  • Read on your device

    Your CSV is parsed in the browser tab. Messages go from your browser straight to the Gmail API.

  • Send-only permission

    Google sign-in asks for one scope, gmail.send. Automailr can send email as you. It can’t read, search or delete anything in your inbox.

  • Encrypted sign-in token

    Your Google access token is encrypted in the browser with AES-256-GCM and expires after 55 minutes. Signing out deletes the key.

AI polish is optional. If you use it, your subject and message, with the {{fields}} still in place, go to Google’s Gemini API. Your recipient list doesn’t.

Limits

Worth knowing before you upload a big list.

  • 5,000

    rows per CSV. Split bigger lists into batches.

  • 25MB

    of attachments per message, in total. That’s Gmail’s cap.

  • ~500

    emails a day from a personal Gmail account.

  • ~2,000

    emails a day from a Google Workspace account.

The daily caps are Google’s, not Automailr’s. They count everything you send from that account in a day, and Google can change them.
